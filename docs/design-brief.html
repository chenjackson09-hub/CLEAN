Chapter 1

The app

In one paragraph

Clean is a two-sided marketplace for home cleaning. Hosts (people who need a home, villa, guesthouse or office cleaned) pick a day on a calendar, see which approved cleaners are free and within range, and send a booking request. Cleaners mark the days and hours they are available, answer requests within 24 hours, and chat with the host once matched. Every person is approved by an admin before they can take part. Payment happens between the two people outside the app. It is bilingual (Hebrew right to left, English left to right) and mobile-first.

Name. The product shows "Clean" as its wordmark in the app. The legal text and the signup agreement call it "Cindy Clean". "Cindy" is also the friendly assistant who greets new users. A domain, cindyclean.co.il, is planned. Gap The final public name has not been fixed, and there is no Hebrew name. This brief uses "Clean" and notes where "Cindy" matters.

The problem it solves

This is inferred from how the product is built, not from market research.

  • For hosts: finding a cleaner who is actually free on the day you need, and who someone has checked. The app starts from the day, not from a list of strangers.
  • For cleaners: getting requests that already fit their calendar, their area and their rate, without negotiating over messages first.
  • For both: sharing as little personal information as possible until a match is made. Hosts show a town, not a street. Cleaners show an age, not a birth date.

Gap No customer interviews, competitor research or market sizing have been done. Anything below about "what makes us different" comes from the product's own rules.

Who uses it

Hosts Confirmed

  • Households, plus homes described as apartment, house, villa, guesthouse (including Airbnb) or office
  • Must be 18 or older
  • Many will use Hebrew; some English
  • Optional details: pets, floors, people at home, private home photos

Cleaners Confirmed

  • Individual cleaners (18 or older) who set their own rate, area and available days
  • Offer services such as regular, deep, windows, laundry, move-in or move-out, post-renovation, Pesach, Airbnb, offices, pet-friendly, organizing
  • Hebrew or English

Admins Confirmed

  • The small team running the pilot
  • Approve every host and cleaner, answer support messages, and open or close service areas
  • Internal tool, not a brand surface

Gap We do not have a profile of the typical host or cleaner (age range, income, how comfortable with apps). This matters for tone and type size.

Unique value

  • Calendar first. The host's main screen is a month calendar showing which days are booked, requested or have cleaners free. Search filters were deliberately removed.
  • Everyone is approved. Admins review every sign-up. A separate "Cindy Verified" badge appears only when an admin personally verifies a person. Nothing is verified automatically.
  • Privacy in stages. Hosts' street addresses and home notes reach a cleaner only when a request is sent or accepted. Home photos are private and visible only to a cleaner with a live request. Phone numbers are never shown; people use in-app chat.
  • Flexible days. A host can offer several candidate days for one clean. Accepting one closes the rest.
  • Opened area by area. The service opens locality by locality. A host in a closed area sees a "coming soon" banner but keeps their calendar.
  • A named helper. "Cindy" speaks in the welcome and waitlist messages, giving the product a human voice.

Current visual state

The app has no designed identity yet. It uses plain white cards with large rounded corners, a standard blue as the main action colour, grey text, and system fonts. The admin area uses a stakeholder-supplied palette: teal #75C9C8, blue #80A1D4, lilac #C0B9DD, pale #DED9E2, cream #F7F4EA. The calendar already has a three-colour status language: free = blue, pending = orange, booked = green. A new identity must keep these three states instantly distinguishable.

How it differs from other apps

From the product's own rules, not from a competitor review: day-first calendar discovery, approval by people, staged privacy, flexible-day requests, area-by-area opening and a full Hebrew right-to-left experience. Gap Which cleaning marketplaces the target users already know has not been researched. Do that before claiming any difference publicly.

Emotions the app should create Proposed

These are design goals, chosen to match the product's rules.

  • Trust. "A person looked at this cleaner, and my address is safe."
  • Calm. A home task that feels simple and finished, not a chore list.
  • Warmth. A friendly local helper (Cindy), not a call centre.
  • Freshness. The lightness of a just-cleaned room: air, light, clear surfaces.
  • Belonging. A small community in a place people know, the Hula Valley.

Open questions for the designers' first call

  • Final product name, and a Hebrew name or transliteration.
  • Is the Hula Valley an identity element, or only the first place we open? (Affects whether the brand can travel to other regions.)
  • Who is the primary audience for the brand: hosts or cleaners? The app serves both.
  • Is there existing photography, or will it be commissioned or illustrated?
  • Should the helper "Cindy" become a visible character? Today she is only a name in messages.
  • Any colours or symbols to avoid (religious, political or regional sensitivities)?
Chapter 2

Preply and Komoot: what to borrow

The goal is an original identity. Both apps are used here as lessons in how to organise and present information, not as styles to copy.

Preply: how a marketplace of people feels friendly

  • Person cards that do the selling. A cleaner card should work the way a tutor card does: face first, one line of who they are, the price, the rating, one clear button.
  • Visual hierarchy. One strong number (price), one trust signal (rating), one action per card.
  • Profiles as a story. Photo, short intro, facts in tidy tiles, reviews. Our cleaner profile already follows this order.
  • Friendly onboarding. One question per screen, small progress indicator, playful tone. Our wizards already work this way and need a better skin.
  • Playful but professional. Rounded shapes, bright accent, warm language, no clutter.

Komoot: how place and discovery feel inspiring

  • Place as the hero. Our launch is a real, loved landscape. Locality names, distance and the service-area circle can feel like part of a journey, not a form field.
  • Outdoor palette. Greens, water blues and earth tones give the product a sense of fresh air.
  • Photography and storytelling. Real places and real people, used generously on empty states, onboarding and area pages.
  • Clear detail. Information-dense screens that stay legible: a tidy stats row, a map, then text.
  • Mobile navigation. A thumb-friendly layout where the map or calendar owns the screen and details slide up as a sheet.

What not to take. Preply's marketplace pressure (urgency banners, heavy filtering) does not suit a calm, approval-based pilot. Komoot's dense route-planning tools do not apply, because we do not plan routes. Keep the feeling, drop the machinery.

Chapter 3

Three brand directions

All three must work in Hebrew right to left and English left to right, so every type recommendation uses families on Google Fonts that include both scripts. All three keep the free, pending and booked calendar states distinct.

A · Valley Morning

Komoot-leaning

Concept. The Hula Valley at first light: wetland water, green hills and the pale outline of Mount Hermon. Clean feels like fresh air coming into a room.

Personality
Calm, local, trustworthy, quietly proud of place. Speaks plainly and warmly.
Colours
    Typography
    Headings: Rubik (600), friendly and slightly rounded, with a matching Hebrew. Body: Heebo (400 and 500). Numbers and prices: Rubik tabular. All three include Hebrew.
    Logo
    A rising sun over layered hills, the lower layer reading as water. Pairs with logo concept 3 or 4.
    Icons
    Rounded line icons, 2 px stroke, a few filled accents in amber. Place icons (hill, water, pin) used sparingly.
    Imagery
    Real photography of the valley in soft morning light, plus very light, desaturated landscape bands for backgrounds. People photographed in daylight, at home, in natural poses. Soft hand-drawn contour lines as a recurring texture.
    UI language
    White cards on a misty background, 16 px corners, thin tinted borders instead of heavy shadows. A contour-line texture behind headers. Buttons in valley green, with water teal for secondary.
    Why it fits
    It matches the launch place, feels clean without looking clinical, and gives the strongest Komoot-style sense of discovery. Risk: ties the brand to one region.

    B · Bright Hello

    Preply-leaning

    Concept. A friendly neighbour who always says hello first. Bold colour, round shapes and a character. Cindy is the face of the brand.

    Personality
    Cheerful, helpful, playful but professional. Uses short sentences and the occasional smile.
    Colours
      Typography
      Headings: Secular One, chunky and friendly in Hebrew and Latin, used large and sparingly. Body and UI: Assistant (400, 600). Large type sizes for readability.
      Logo
      A speech bubble or rounded sparkle shape that becomes Cindy's face. Pairs with logo concept 5.
      Icons
      Chunky filled icons with rounded ends in two tones (blue plus mint).
      Imagery
      Flat, friendly illustration of Cindy and everyday home scenes. Cut-out photos of real cleaners on colour blocks. Confetti-like sparkles on success moments.
      UI language
      Pill buttons, 24 px card corners, colour-blocked card headers, sticker-style badges for Cindy Verified. Micro-animations on success.
      Why it fits
      It makes sign-up fun, matches the "Cindy" helper already in the copy, and is the closest to Preply's feel. Risk: can feel like a children's app if over-used, and less rooted in place.

      C · Daylight Glass

      Precise and premium

      Concept. The look of a window after cleaning: clarity, reflection, a single bright highlight. Minimal, architectural, confident.

      Personality
      Crisp, precise, reliable, understated. Speaks briefly and exactly.
      Colours
        Typography
        Headings: Heebo 800 with tight spacing, set large. Body and UI: IBM Plex Sans Hebrew (400, 500), which also covers Latin. Generous white space.
        Logo
        A sharp geometric mark: a square window pane with a single diagonal glint. Pairs with logo concept 2 or a refined version of 1.
        Icons
        Thin 1.5 px geometric line icons on a strict grid, no fills.
        Imagery
        Architectural interior photography with natural light, clean surfaces, minimal objects. Duotone treatment (navy and glass blue) for people photos.
        UI language
        Hairline borders, 8 px corners, large type, lots of air, one lemon accent per screen. Soft light-reflection gradient on key surfaces.
        Why it fits
        It signals cleanliness and reliability most directly and suits guesthouses and offices. Risk: coldest of the three, and least like Preply or Komoot.

        Calendar states. Whatever direction is chosen, free, pending and booked must differ by more than hue (add an icon or pattern) so they work for colour-blind users and in small month cells. Check each palette above for this before approving it.

        Chapter 4

        Five logo concepts

        Every concept needs a Latin wordmark and a Hebrew wordmark that look like the same brand. The Hebrew name is not decided, so concepts show the symbol first. Test each as a rounded-square app icon at 48 px and as a 16 px browser-tab icon.

        1

        The Sparkle C

        Idea. A bold letter C whose opening holds a four-point sparkle, like light on a clean surface.

        Symbolism. C for Clean and Cindy; the sparkle means "just cleaned".

        Shape. One thick rounded stroke forming the C, with a small sparkle placed in the gap. Works in one colour.

        App icon. White C and sparkle on a solid brand colour.

        Small sizes. Strong. The C alone still reads at 16 px; the sparkle may merge, so draw a simplified version.

        Pairs with. Directions A, B or C.

        2

        Window Glint

        Idea. A square pane with a single diagonal highlight cutting across one corner.

        Symbolism. Clear glass, a home (window), light.

        Shape. A rounded square outline, one thick diagonal line, one small tick. Pure geometry.

        App icon. Works as a negative space cut in a coloured square.

        Small sizes. Very strong; extremely simple. Least distinctive, since window marks are common.

        Pairs with. Direction C.

        3

        Valley Horizon

        Idea. A circle (sun) above two soft overlapping hills, the lower band drawn as a gentle wipe or wave.

        Symbolism. The Hula Valley, fresh morning, and a cloth wiping a surface in one stroke.

        Shape. A circle and two flowing bands, all within a rounded square. Two or three colours.

        App icon. Sun in amber over green and teal bands on mist.

        Small sizes. Good if reduced to the circle plus one band at 16 px.

        Pairs with. Direction A.

        4

        House Pin

        Idea. A map pin whose inside is a simple house outline with a small sparkle at the roof.

        Symbolism. A cleaner coming to a place; location plus home.

        Shape. A teardrop pin with a house cut out of it, drawn with one outline weight.

        App icon. Pin centred on a solid square, white on colour.

        Small sizes. Recognisable, but pins are common in many apps, so distinctiveness depends on the house and sparkle detail.

        Pairs with. Directions A and C.

        5

        Cindy Bubble

        Idea. A rounded speech bubble that is also a friendly, minimal face (two dots and a small smile) with a sparkle on the corner.

        Symbolism. The helper Cindy; chat and welcome; a human voice.

        Shape. A soft squircle bubble with a small tail, two dot eyes, a gentle curve.

        App icon. Bubble on a bright colour; the tail points to the lower corner.

        Small sizes. The bubble silhouette holds; the face may disappear at 16 px, which is acceptable.

        Pairs with. Direction B. It only works if Cindy becomes a visible character. Gap

        Selection criteria. Pick for: reads at 16 px, works in one colour, looks the same mirrored in a right-to-left layout, is not already a common stock symbol, and says something about this product.

        Chapter 5

        UX and UI architecture

        This chapter keeps today's structure and recommends how to present it. Items marked Proposed are design suggestions, not decided features.

        Navigation structure Confirmed

        Host

        Two-row top header. Row 1: wordmark, notification bell, menu button, profile photo. Row 2 tabs: Home, Schedule, Bookings, Chat. Profile is the photo, not a tab.

        Cleaner

        Same header. Tabs: Home, Requests, Schedule, Chat. Red badge on Requests for pending answers.

        Admin

        Top bar plus left sidebar (a menu on phones). Dashboard, applications, requests, matches, cleaners, hosts, support, locations.

        Proposed On phones, consider moving the four tabs to a bottom bar for thumb reach, with the header reduced to wordmark, bell and photo. In right-to-left, the order mirrors.

        Home screens

        • Host home Confirmed: greeting, a Today and tomorrow strip, "Waiting for an answer" with the time left, "Coming up", and a collapsed "Past cleans". With no bookings, a single button to find a cleaner.
        • Cleaner home Confirmed: greeting and search, an amber "requests are waiting" link, "Finished? Mark as complete", Today and tomorrow, Upcoming, collapsed Past.
        • Proposed Make the next clean a large hero card with date, cleaner photo and one action (open chat). Add a regional image in the header that changes with the season or time of day.

        Discovery: calendar first, optionally map-aware

        • Today Confirmed: the host opens the Schedule calendar. Day cells show booked hours, "N asked", and blue dots for free cleaners. Tapping a day opens a sheet of free cleaner cards. There are no search filters.
        • Preply-style card Proposed: large photo, "First L.", rating and clean count, town and distance, price per hour, available time chips, Cindy Verified, one primary button "Request".
        • Komoot-style map layer Proposed: inside the day sheet, a toggle between list and a map of the area. The host's town is marked, cleaners appear as photo pins at their town (never an exact address), and the sheet rises from the bottom. Needs a decision, because it is a new feature. Privacy rule to keep: pins show towns only.

        Profiles

        • Cleaner profile Confirmed: top bar, photo, name, rating, rate tile, three stat tiles, languages, About, services as pills, preferences, host reviews. Proposed: add the service-area map as a full-width image band with the radius, and a sticky "Request" button.
        • Host profile (seen by a cleaner) Confirmed: intro paragraph, home tiles, pets, typical needs, reviews, with home photos and notes locked until a live request.

        Booking request and core workflow

        1. Host taps a day, then a cleaner, then "Request". Confirmed
        2. One request card: date, time, duration, cleaning type, extras, what matters, pets, notes, address. It becomes a read-only sent card. Confirmed
        3. Cleaner sees one row per host, opens it, accepts in one tap. A 24-hour countdown shows time left. Confirmed
        4. Chat opens automatically with a booking card; a reminder arrives the day before. Confirmed
        5. Cancellation is a single shared sheet with a reason. Confirmed
        6. Proposed Give each state a clear visual stage (Requested, Accepted, Done, Rated) as a small progress track on the booking card.

        Onboarding

        • Structure Confirmed: account first, then 9 cleaner or 7 host screens with a "Step X of N" bar, fixed Back and Continue bar, optional "Skip for now", a final preview with Edit on each section, exit with resume.
        • Proposed Show Cindy as a small guide on each screen, with one friendly sentence. Use a landscape or home illustration that gently changes per step. Celebrate the finish with a short moment, then the waitlist message for hosts.

        Mobile-first principles Proposed

        • One primary action per screen; primary buttons within thumb reach and at least 44 px tall.
        • Bottom sheets for details so the calendar or list stays visible behind.
        • Right-to-left is a first-class layout: design in Hebrew first, then check English. Use start and end, never left and right. Icons that imply direction (arrows, back) must mirror.
        • Body text no smaller than 16 px; Hebrew needs slightly more line height than Latin.
        • Photos first, but always with a fallback (initial on a colour) because some people have no photo yet.
        • Never show a phone number or street address on a card.

        How the two inspirations work together. Preply gives the product its people-first cards, profiles and friendly onboarding. Komoot gives it place: the calendar day opens onto an area, the cleaner profile shows where they work, and photography ties both to the Hula Valley. The common thread is one clear action per screen, a trust signal beside every person, and a sense of local belonging.

        Chapter 7

        Recommendation

        Start with Direction A, Valley Morning, using the friendly components of Direction B.

        Why it represents the product. The product opens one real place at a time, starting in the Hula Valley. Water, green hills and morning light say "clean" and "local" at once, and they give a small pilot a story no generic cleaning brand has.

        How it combines Preply and Komoot. The cards, profiles, rounded buttons and one-question onboarding borrow Preply's warmth. The palette, the landscape imagery, the service-area map and the sheet that rises over the calendar borrow Komoot's sense of place.

        What makes it distinctive. A real regional identity (hill and water motifs, contour lines), a calm approach to trust (the verified badge and staged privacy shown visually), and Cindy as a gentle guide in onboarding and empty states.

        Risks to manage. A regional symbol could limit expansion, so keep the logo abstract (a horizon and a sun, not a named mountain). Greens and teals can look similar in small calendar cells, so the status icons are mandatory. Warmth should not turn childish; borrow B's roundness, not its mascot-heavy tone, until Cindy's role as a character is decided.
